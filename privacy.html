Ledger

Privacy

Ledger is a private planning app used by one household. This page explains what it keeps, where it is kept, and who can see it. Updated 26 October 2026.

What Ledger keeps

  • Your account: your email address and name, used to sign you in.
  • What you put in: your plans, actions, notes, requests at Home, and anything else you enter. This includes photos you choose to add.
  • Notifications: if you turn them on, a code for this device so a notification can reach it, plus the device's name (e.g. "iPhone app").

Ledger has no adverts, no tracking and no analytics. Nothing is sold or shared for marketing.

Where it is kept

On Google Firebase (sign-in, the database and the notification service), and in a copy on your own device so Ledger works offline. The website itself is served by Vercel. Push notifications are delivered through Apple's and Google's notification services.

Who can see it

  • Your own Ledger is visible only to you.
  • Things at Home: a request, task or ongoing task is seen by the people it involves. If it is shared with Home, everyone at Home can see it.
  • What the owner chooses to share with a partner, and nothing else.

Access is enforced by the database's own security rules, not just by the app.

Removing your data

Signing out removes this device from notifications. To have your account and everything in it deleted, ask Shaun, who runs Ledger. It will be removed.
